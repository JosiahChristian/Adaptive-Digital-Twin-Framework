Selective change-point detection produced a strong risk–coverage relationship for event localization. Increasing acceptance selectivity reduced timing error and substantially increased exact and near-exact event localization. At the highest tested confidence level, exact localization exceeded 80% and within-three-step localization exceeded 90% across the evaluated horizons. However, improved localization confidence did not translate monotonically into improved overall epistemic inference. High selectivity produced low conditional epistemic error among accepted events, but uniform fallback on the majority of trajectories yielded overall epistemic MAE that was generally worse than the uniform-memory baseline. Thus detector confidence measures whether an event location is trustworthy, but does not determine whether adopting event-anchored memory is beneficial for the current trajectory.

\# Experiment 083 — Seed-Level Support-Aware Failure Decomposition



\## Objective



Experiment 082 established that support-aware safe-action expansion improves responsiveness across independent generated datasets, but does not preserve primary-gate consequence performance uniformly.



At the conservative support operating point



\\\[

\\tau\_s=2.50,

\\]



the multi-seed controller improved mean responsive-action retention and reduced over-persistence, but also increased mean regret and under-persistence.



Experiment 083 therefore analyzes the already-generated seed-level validation results from Experiment 082 without retraining or changing any controller parameter.



The purpose is to distinguish two hypotheses:



\\\[

\\boxed{

H\_1:

\\text{the remaining safety cost is concentrated in a few difficult seeds}

}

\\]



versus



\\\[

\\boxed{

H\_2:

\\text{the remaining safety cost is systematically distributed across seeds}.

}

\\]



The central question is



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{Is support-aware degradation driven by rare distribution shifts}

\\atop

\\text{or by a recurring expansion bias across generated populations?}

}

\\]



\---

\## Systematic Rather Than Rare Cost



If the support-aware safety degradation were driven by only one or two unusual populations, most seeds would be expected to fall into the zero-cost category.



Instead:



\\\[

\\boxed{

7/10

}

\\]

\\\[

\\text{responsive improvement}

\\]

\\\[

\\text{additional regret}.

Therefore the remaining consequence cost is not primarily a rare outlier phenomenon.



It is distributed across the majority of generated populations.



The evidence therefore favors:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{systematic expansion bias}.

\\]

\# Descriptive Correlation Results



Because only ten seeds are available, these correlations are descriptive and should not be interpreted as causal estimates.



Nevertheless, they reveal a striking structure.



\---



\## Responsive Gain Versus Regret



The correlation between responsive-retention gain and regret increase was:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\rho(

\\Delta A\_{\\text{responsive}},

\\Delta R

)

=

+0.113.

}

\\]



This is weak.



Therefore larger responsiveness improvements are not strongly associated with larger regret penalties across seeds.



This is encouraging.



It suggests that responsiveness gain itself is not the dominant source of consequence degradation.



\---



\## Harmful Expansions Versus Regret



The correlation between harmful expansion count and regret increase was:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\rho(

N\_{\\text{harmful}},

\\Delta R

)

=

+0.977.

}

\\]



This is extremely strong.



Seeds with more harmful expansions almost always incur larger increases in regret.



Thus the aggregate consequence cost is highly localized to the harmful expansion mechanism rather than to expansion generally.

\\]

This indicates that the harmful expansions measured by the diagnostic correspond directly to the controller's extra under-persistence decisions.

\# Architectural Implication



The results separate the two effects of expansion.



Beneficial expansion produces:



\\\[

\\text{responsiveness gain}

\\]



with little relationship to aggregate regret increase.



Harmful expansion produces:



\\\[

\\text{under-persistence}

\\]



and strongly predicts regret degradation.



Therefore the next objective should not be to suppress expansion globally.



It should be:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{preserve beneficial responsive recovery}

\\atop

\\text{while selectively identifying harmful expansion contexts}.

}

\\]

\# Rejection of the Rare-Shift Hypothesis



Experiment 080 initially suggested that the harmful seed-44000 cases were support-extrapolation failures.



Experiment 082 showed that support distance substantially reduces harmful expansion across new seeds.



Experiment 083 now reveals that residual harmful expansions still occur in:



\\\[

7/10

\\]



validation populations.



Therefore the remaining failures cannot be explained solely by occasional extreme distribution shift.



Instead:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{support distance removes one important failure mode}

}

\\]



but



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{a recurring harmful-expansion mechanism remains}.

}

\\]



\---



\# Systematic Expansion Bias



The evidence supports the interpretation that support-aware expansion still has a systematic false-positive bias.



The mechanism frequently succeeds:



\\\[

\\text{beneficial expansions}

>

\\text{harmful expansions}

\\]



on most seeds.



However, a small number of false-positive expansions remain in many populations.



Because each harmful expansion can produce meaningful regret, these few errors dominate the aggregate consequence penalty.



This is a classic asymmetric decision problem.



Many correct expansions provide responsiveness value.



A small number of incorrect expansions create most of the safety cost.



\---



\# Why Global Threshold Tightening Is Not the Immediate Next Step



One possible reaction would be to make the support threshold even smaller.



Experiment 083 does not justify doing so yet.



The weak correlations between responsiveness gain and regret indicate that aggressive global shrinkage could discard many beneficial expansions unnecessarily.



The better research question is whether harmful expansions possess a common context-level signature that distinguishes them from beneficial expansions.



Therefore the next experiment should analyze the pooled expansion events directly.



\---

\## Next Research Direction



Experiment 084 should perform a pooled cross-seed harmful-expansion feature decomposition.



The controller must remain frozen at:



\\\[

\\tau\_p=0.60,

\\]



\\\[

\\tau\_d=0.020,

\\]



and:



\\\[

\\tau\_s=2.50.

\\]



The same validation seeds:



\\\[

44001\\text{--}44010

\\]



should be regenerated only to recover context-level diagnostics that were not saved by Experiment 082.



No threshold should be tuned.



Every actual action-changing expansion should be labeled as:



\\\[

\\text{beneficial}

\\]



or



\\\[

\\text{harmful}.

\\]



The pooled analysis should compare:



\- safety probability,

\- predicted downside,

\- support distance,

\- action-step size,

\- predicted-regret margin,

\- predicted loss differences,

\- predicted under-persistence risk,

\- base context features,

\- realized incremental regret,

\- and true responsive action.



The experiment should report:



\- beneficial and harmful sample counts,

\- mean and median feature values,

\- standardized effect sizes,

\- feature-level separation,

\- and harmful-context ranges.



The central question becomes:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{Do residual harmful expansions share a stable cross-seed}

\\atop

\\text{feature signature that is absent from beneficial recoveries?}

}

\\]



If such a signature exists, the next stage can test it prospectively on a new untouched generation-seed set rather than continuing to tune on the current validation population.

\# Experiment 102 — Prospective Calibration-Guard Veto Selectivity Decomposition



\## Purpose



Experiment 101 demonstrated prospectively that a frozen historical calibration-risk guard can reduce harmful adaptive expansions and regret on an untouched controller seed block.



However, the intervention also vetoed beneficial adaptive expansions.



Experiment 102 examines the veto population generated by Experiment 101 to determine whether the calibration-risk score itself provides sufficient within-veto selectivity to distinguish harmful from beneficial support-admitted expansions.



This is a retrospective diagnostic analysis of already-observed Experiment 101 events.



It does \*\*not\*\* modify the controller.



It does \*\*not\*\* evaluate new prospective seeds.



It does \*\*not\*\* fit or select a replacement controller threshold.



\---



\## Source Experiment



Experiment 101:



Frozen Calibration-Aware Controller Intervention



Historical calibration seeds:



44001-44090



Prospective controller seeds:



44091-44110



Frozen calibration-risk threshold:



tau\_cal = 0.468010308717



The threshold was selected from historical leave-one-generation-seed-out predictions before the Experiment 101 prospective block was evaluated.



\---



\## Experiment 101 Veto Population



The calibration-aware controller changed the support-admitted action for:



22 expansion events



Of these:



\- beneficial vetoes: 17

\- harmful vetoes: 5



Therefore, the Experiment 101 guard successfully removed harmful adaptations but also suppressed a substantially larger population of beneficial adaptations.



Experiment 102 asks whether those two veto classes can be separated using information already associated with the Experiment 101 events.



\---



\## Primary Diagnostic Question



Within the population already vetoed by the frozen Experiment 101 calibration guard:



\*\*Does increasing calibration-risk probability preferentially concentrate harmful expansions relative to beneficial expansions?\*\*



If so, a stricter calibration threshold might provide a simple path toward greater intervention selectivity.



If not, calibration probability should not be treated as a monotonic measure of harmfulness within the already-flagged population.



\---



\## Event Fields Examined



The decomposition evaluates:



\- calibration\_probability\_baseline\_action

\- context\_support\_distance

\- predicted\_baseline\_action\_loss

\- baseline\_action\_loss\_error

\- baseline\_regret



The first three quantities are available as pre-action or prediction-time descriptors.



The final two quantities are realized-outcome diagnostics and are used only to characterize what happened after the baseline action was evaluated.



They must not be interpreted as deployable pre-action selectors.

\## Calibration-Probability Threshold Geometry



The original frozen threshold:



tau\_cal = 0.468010308717



flagged all 22 Experiment 101 veto events:



\- harmful captured: 5/5

\- beneficial captured: 17/17

\- harmful recall: 100%

\- beneficial preservation: 0%

\- harmful precision: 22.727%



Raising the threshold did not produce the desired monotonic selectivity.



At:



tau = 0.500000



the rule retained:



\- harmful: 4

\- beneficial: 17



Therefore, one harmful event was lost before any beneficial veto was recovered.



At approximately:



tau = 0.539440



the rule retained:



\- harmful: 2

\- beneficial: 14



with:



\- harmful recall: 40%

\- beneficial preservation: 17.647%



At:



tau = 0.600000

\- harmful: 2

\- beneficial: 11

\- harmful recall: 40%

\- beneficial preservation: 35.294%



Above approximately:



tau = 0.654791



no harmful vetoes remained in the flagged population.



Six beneficial vetoes still remained.



Thus, progressively increasing the calibration-risk threshold removes harmful coverage faster than it recovers beneficial adaptation.

\---



\## Primary Diagnostic Result



Experiment 102 falsifies the simple selectivity hypothesis that:



\*\*raising the Experiment 101 calibration-risk threshold should preferentially eliminate beneficial vetoes while retaining harmful vetoes.\*\*



The observed geometry shows the opposite limitation.



Harmful events begin disappearing from the flagged population before most beneficial events are recovered.



Therefore:



\*\*the Experiment 101 selectivity problem cannot be solved simply by increasing the scalar calibration-risk threshold.\*\*



\---



\## Architectural Interpretation



Experiments 099-101 established that historical local calibration information contains useful prospective information about severe consequence underestimation and can improve controller safety when used as an intervention signal.



Experiment 102 refines that conclusion.



The calibration-risk probability appears useful as a first-stage indicator of a broad consequence-model danger regime.



However, within that already-flagged regime, the magnitude of the scalar probability is not sufficient to determine whether an adaptive expansion will ultimately be harmful or beneficial.



This suggests a possible hierarchical interpretation:



1\. Historical calibration state identifies when the consequence model may be unreliable.

2\. A separate representation may be required to determine which candidate adaptations should actually be suppressed within that unreliable regime.



This interpretation remains a hypothesis and requires further testing.



\---



\## Important Negative Result



A stricter calibration threshold is \*\*not\*\* supported as the next controller modification.



Threshold escalation would sacrifice harmful-event coverage before recovering most beneficial adaptations.



Therefore, Experiment 102 does not authorize post hoc replacement of:



tau\_cal = 0.468010308717



with a higher value.



The Experiment 101 frozen intervention result remains unchanged.



\---



\## Next Research Question



The Experiment 101 calibration probability is generated from a multivariate historical representation containing:



\- predicted\_action\_loss

\- local\_mean\_error

\- local\_error\_std

\- local\_underestimate\_fraction

\- local\_severe\_underestimate\_fraction



Two actions can potentially receive similar aggregate calibration-risk probabilities while reaching those probabilities through different combinations of underlying historical calibration features.



The next diagnostic question is therefore:



\*\*Do harmful and beneficial Experiment 101 vetoes differ in the internal pre-action calibration-state geometry that produced their similar aggregate risk probabilities?\*\*



A subsequent experiment should reconstruct the frozen local calibration representation for the 22 Experiment 101 veto events and examine its constituent dimensions directly.



Such an experiment should remain diagnostic before any new controller intervention is proposed.



\---



\## Scientific Transition



Experiment 099 established historical calibration-risk representation.



Experiment 100 demonstrated prospective generalization of that representation.



Experiment 101 demonstrated prospective controller benefit from a frozen calibration-aware intervention.



Experiment 102 demonstrates that the resulting intervention's selectivity limitation cannot be resolved by simple scalar threshold escalation.



The research question therefore advances from:



\*\*Can historical calibration experience identify dangerous adaptation?\*\*



to:



\*\*What structure within historical calibration experience distinguishes harmful adaptation from useful adaptation once the digital twin recognizes that its consequence model is at elevated risk of failure?\*\*

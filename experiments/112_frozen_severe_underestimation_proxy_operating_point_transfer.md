\# Experiment 112 — Frozen Severe-Underestimation Proxy Operating-Point Transfer



\## Purpose



Experiment 111 established that the frozen pre-action severe-underestimation proxy from Experiment 110 carries transferable information about harmful support expansion.



The severe proxy achieved:



\\\[

\\text{mean reciprocal AUC}=0.821

\\]



with:



\\\[

\\text{minimum reciprocal AUC}=0.764.

\\]



This modestly improved on the historical `local\_error\_std` ranking baseline from Experiment 106.



However, better ranking does not imply that a stable intervention operating point exists.



Experiment 112 therefore asks:



\*\*Does the frozen severe-underestimation probability support a more stable cross-block operating threshold than the raw historical error-dispersion signal tested in Experiment 107?\*\*



Experiment 112 is a historical operating-point transfer experiment.



It does not:



\- introduce a new prospective seed block;

\- modify the adaptive controller;

\- define a deployable intervention threshold;

\- or retrain the severe-underestimation representation.



\---

\# Threshold-Selection Rule



Experiment 112 preserves the same safety-oriented historical operating-point rule used in Experiment 107.



Within each training block:



1\. enumerate thresholds observed in the training population;

2\. retain thresholds achieving harmful recall of at least 80%;

3\. among qualifying thresholds, maximize beneficial specificity;

4\. break ties using higher harmful precision;

5\. if still tied, choose the higher threshold.



The selected threshold is then frozen and applied unchanged to the opposite block.



The process is subsequently reversed.



Thus:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{test-block outcomes do not participate in threshold selection.}

}

\\]

\# Why Another Scalar Search Is Not Justified



The research has now evaluated:



\- raw local error dispersion;

\- normalized local error dispersion;

\- frozen severe-underestimation probability;

\- frozen predicted signed calibration error;

\- and combinations among these signals.



Repeatedly, the scalar representations can rank risk better than chance.



Repeatedly, a single context-free threshold fails to provide stable safety/selectivity transfer.



Therefore continued search over scalar cutoffs would risk fitting historical idiosyncrasies rather than addressing the underlying phenomenon.



The research question should now move from:



\*\*Which scalar threshold should the controller use?\*\*



to:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{What pre-action regime information determines}

\\atop

\\text{which decision boundary is appropriate?}

}

\\]

\# What Experiment 112 Supports



Experiment 112 supports the claims that:



1\. the severe-underestimation proxy does not improve historical operating-point transfer over error dispersion;



2\. ranking improvement alone is insufficient for safe threshold deployment;



3\. harmful support expansions occupy different severe-proxy probability regions across historical regimes;



4\. the threshold instability remains after moving from a raw historical statistic to a learned pre-action probability;



5\. the remaining problem is consistent with regime-dependent decision geometry.



\---



\# What Experiment 112 Does Not Support



Experiment 112 does not establish:



1\. a deployable severe-proxy threshold;



2\. superiority of the severe proxy as a threshold variable;



3\. that either historical threshold should be averaged;



4\. that one transfer direction should be privileged post hoc;



5\. a prospective controller modification;



6\. that the regime dependence has yet been explained;



7\. that the selectivity problem has been solved.



\---



\# Scientific Interpretation



Experiment 112 preserves an important distinction:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{risk estimation}

\\neq

\\text{decision calibration}.

}

\\]



The twin can estimate impending calibration-failure risk better than chance.



The resulting risk probability carries useful information about harmful adaptation.



But the action boundary corresponding to a desired safety/selectivity tradeoff remains regime-dependent.



This indicates that a future adaptive controller may require not merely a scalar risk estimate, but a model of \*\*decision context or regime state\*\*.



That possibility must itself be demonstrated before controller design proceeds.

\# Next Research Direction



The next experiment should test whether the operating-point shift is associated with a detectable pre-action regime state.



This should remain diagnostic.



A suitable analysis should ask whether the two historical blocks differ systematically in pre-action variables among support-expansion events and, more importantly, whether those variables alter the relationship between severe-underestimation probability and harmful expansion.



The analysis should distinguish:



\- block identity prediction;

\- harmful-risk prediction;

\- interaction with the frozen severe proxy;

\- pre-action versus outcome-derived information;

\- and whether any candidate regime indicator is stable enough to justify later prospective validation.



The central question becomes:



\*\*Can the digital twin identify, before action execution, which risk-to-action mapping regime it currently occupies?\*\*



No new controller intervention should occur until that question has been answered.

\# Experiment 116 — Independent Historical Action-Harm Replication



\## Purpose



Experiment 115 identified a strong structural association between support-expansion action identity and harmful expansion in the historical seed population `44071–44110`.



In that population:



\\\[

N(H \\mid A=1)=8/33

\\]



while:



\\\[

N(H \\mid A=2)=0/55.

\\]



All eight observed harmful support expansions therefore occurred under action 1.



Because that result was discovered retrospectively, Experiment 116 performs an independent historical replication using an earlier, non-overlapping population selected before its harmful-by-action counts were inspected.



The primary question was frozen before examining the replication outcome:



\\\[

\\boxed{

P(H\\mid A=1)>P(H\\mid A=2)?

}

\\]



Exact reproduction of the Experiment 115 zero-harm action-2 pattern was explicitly defined as a secondary result rather than a requirement for directional replication.

\# Effect Size



The Fisher exact odds-ratio estimate is:



\\\[

\\boxed{2.7708}.

\\]



Thus the observed odds of harmful expansion under action 1 are approximately:



\\\[

2.77\\times

\\]



the observed odds under action 2 in this independent historical population.



This is an effect-size estimate rather than evidence of causal action effects.



\---



\# Statistical Uncertainty



The two-sided Fisher exact test gives:



\\\[

\\boxed{p=0.112202}.

\\]



Therefore the independent replication does not reach the conventional:



\\\[

p<0.05

\\]



significance threshold.



The Wilson intervals also overlap:



\\\[

A1:

19.150\\%\\text{–}58.960\\%

\\]



\\\[

A2:

9.086\\%\\text{–}30.723\\%.

\\]



Accordingly, Experiment 116 should not be described as statistically decisive.



The appropriate interpretation is:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{directionally replicated with a substantial estimated effect,}

}

\\]



while:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{remaining statistically uncertain at the present sample size.}

}

\\]

\# Secondary Exact-Pattern Replication

Experiment 116 observes:



\\\[

N(H\\mid A=2)=8.

\\]

\\\[

\# Relationship to the Operating-Regime Problem



Experiments 107–114 repeatedly demonstrated instability in attempts to derive a transferable scalar operating point or continuous pre-action regime representation.



Experiment 115 identified action identity as a potentially important structural variable.



Experiment 116 strengthens that interpretation because the direction of the action-harm association survives in an earlier, independent historical population.



However, the absolute harmful rates differ:



\\\[

24.242\\%\\rightarrow36.842\\%

\\]



for action 1, and:



\\\[

0\\%\\rightarrow17.391\\%

\\]



for action 2.



Therefore action identity does not eliminate historical regime dependence.



Instead, the evidence is consistent with:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{action identity being one component of a broader}

\\atop

\\text{state-dependent harmful-expansion mechanism.}

}

\\]

\# Relationship to Severe-Underestimation Risk



Experiment 115 showed that action identity substantially improved pooled discrimination when combined with the frozen severe-underestimation proxy.



However, within-action-1 severe-proxy transfer remained unstable.



Experiment 116 does not test or refit that proxy.



Its purpose is narrower:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{independently replicate the structural action-harm association.}

}

\\]



Therefore no claim is made here that the Experiment 115 action × severe-proxy interaction itself has independently replicated.



That remains a separate hypothesis.



\---



\# Causal Boundary



Experiment 116 is observational and retrospective.



The result:



\\\[

P(H\\mid A=1)>P(H\\mid A=2)

\\]



does not establish:



\\\[

A=1

\\Rightarrow

\\text{causes harm}.

\\]



Action identity may encode or correlate with:



\- state geometry;

\- model uncertainty;

\- action availability;

\- support structure;

\- controller state;

\- latent operating regime;

\- or other pre-action conditions.



Therefore action identity is currently interpreted as a structural risk marker, not a demonstrated causal mechanism.



\---



\# What Experiment 116 Supports



Experiment 116 supports the claims that:



1\. the Experiment 115 action-harm direction replicates in an independent earlier historical population;



2\. action 1 has a higher observed harmful-expansion rate than action 2 in seeds `44001–44010`;



3\. the independent harmful rates are 36.842% for action 1 and 17.391% for action 2;



4\. the observed rate difference is +19.451 percentage points;



5\. the estimated Fisher odds ratio is approximately 2.77;



6\. harmful action-1 events occur across multiple seeds;



7\. harmful action-2 events also occur across multiple seeds;



8\. action identity deserves continued treatment as a structural risk variable.



\---



\# What Experiment 116 Does Not Support



Experiment 116 does not establish:



1\. conventional statistical significance for the action association;



2\. that action 1 causes harmful expansion;



3\. that action 2 is safe;



4\. that harmful expansion occurs exclusively under action 1;



5\. a controller threshold;



6\. an action-specific veto rule;



7\. prospective controller improvement;



8\. replication of the Experiment 115 severe-proxy × action interaction;



9\. a complete explanation of historical operating-point instability.



\---



\# Scientific Boundary

\# Next Research Direction



Experiment 116 changes the research question.



The relevant hypothesis is no longer:



\\\[

\\text{Is action 2 universally safe?}

\\]



That hypothesis is directly contradicted by the independent replication population.



The stronger remaining question is:



\\\[

\\boxed{

\\text{Why does action 1 carry consistently higher relative}

\\atop

\\text{harmful-expansion risk across historical populations?}

}

\\]



A subsequent experiment should distinguish between two possibilities:



1\. \*\*action identity itself carries stable structural information\*\*, or



2\. \*\*action identity is acting as a proxy for pre-action state/support conditions that differ systematically when action 1 is selected.\*\*



The next analysis should therefore compare pre-action state and support geometry between action-1 and action-2 expansions across both independent historical populations while keeping realized outcome information excluded from representation construction.



Only after that mechanism is better understood should an action-conditioned prospective controller modification be considered.

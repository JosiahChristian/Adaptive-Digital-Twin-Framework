\begin{abstract}
Adaptive decision pipelines can exhibit strong statistical patterns that do not necessarily identify the mechanism that produced them or provide information available at decision time. We examine two such problems in a simulated adaptive digital-twin pipeline: localization of ranking changes near a fixed top-N cutoff and discrimination of harmful action-space expansion outcomes. In Experiment 166, exclusion-membership changes were strongly concentrated near the cutoff in the poisoning condition, but a prospectively frozen label-preserving non-poison perturbation matched the ranking perturbation magnitude and reproduced essentially the same localization pattern. The poison-minus-control localization difference was 0.001845 with a 95\% seed-bootstrap interval of [0.0000, 0.00554], so the frozen poisoning-specificity criterion was not met. A separate compact harmful-expansion model achieved approximately 0.979 ROC AUC retrospectively, but two of its three features incorporate post-outcome quantities and therefore cannot support a pre-decision prediction claim. Models restricted to temporally legitimate loss-surface information showed weaker exploratory discrimination. Together, the results support a pipeline-specific cutoff-localization phenomenon and motivate strict separation of observed association, perturbation specificity, causal mechanism, and prediction-time validity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Adaptive digital-twin research increasingly couples continuously updated digital representations with prediction, decision support, and adaptive control. Recent implementations span self-adaptive manufacturing architectures, adaptive control systems, and digital-twin-supported decision systems \cite{Splettstosser2025,AutonomousDT2024,DecisionDT2024}. These examples motivate treating the digital twin not only as a state representation, but also as part of a decision pipeline whose validity depends on how predictions are converted into interventions.

Predictive accuracy and downstream decision quality are not interchangeable objectives. Predict-then-optimize and decision-focused learning study settings in which predicted quantities are subsequently used inside an optimization or discrete decision problem \cite{Elmachtoub2022,Wilder2019,Mandi2023}. Fixed-budget ranking also creates a sensitivity question near the selection boundary: top-K theory identifies separation around the K-th and (K+1)-th items as central to reliable set identification \cite{Chen2015}, while ranking-stability work studies how changes in scoring rules alter ranked outputs \cite{Asudeh2018}. These results make near-boundary sensitivity a plausible competing explanation for cutoff-localized membership changes, but do not establish that explanation as the cause of Experiment 166.

A separate validity problem arises when a predictor contains information unavailable at the intended prediction time. Methodological work on leakage documents how outcome-linked or otherwise unavailable information can yield overoptimistic performance estimates \cite{Kapoor2023}. Leakage in the harmful-expansion model here is established by the model's own feature definitions and timing audit, not by external literature.

This study therefore focuses on claim validity rather than maximizing favorable metrics. The experimental record includes preregistered positive findings, failed prospective replications, null or negative mechanism tests, matched non-poison controls, and a later timing-leakage audit. The manuscript asks whether Experiment 166 identifies a poisoning-specific mechanism and whether the strongest harmful-expansion classifier is genuinely available before decision time. External literature provides context only; internal committed artifacts adjudicate these questions.

\subsection{Experiment 166: fixed-budget ranking and cutoff localization}
Experiment 166 evaluated changes in exclusion membership within a fixed top-N ranking/intervention pipeline. It used the frozen source population, features \texttt{action\_2}, \texttt{action\_3}, and \texttt{context\_support\_distance}, a class-balanced logistic-regression hazard model, and 20\% targeted unsafe-to-safe source-label concealment. Intervention coverage was determined from the clean source model using the frozen 80\% source-unsafe-recall rule, and clean and poisoned models received the same exclusion count within each target seed. A fresh untouched target population of 40 generation seeds (44791--44830) was generated after preregistration.

A membership switch was a candidate excluded by exactly one of the clean or poisoned models. Candidates were ranked within seed by absolute clean cutoff margin; the primary near-cutoff band was the closest 10\% of candidate rows. Criterion 1 used seed-stratified $2\times2$ counts and a Mantel--Haenszel common odds ratio with a two-sided Cochran--Mantel--Haenszel test. Criterion 2 computed per-seed \texttt{net\_unsafe\_crossing} and \texttt{delta\_unsafe\_selected}, then estimated their Spearman association with 10,000 bootstrap resamples of whole generation seeds.

The poisoning condition had mean exclusion-set Jaccard overlap 0.923823 and 308 membership switches. The original cutoff-band analysis yielded a Mantel--Haenszel common odds ratio of 10.567477 with 95\% CI [8.345537, 13.380992]. The seed-level association had Spearman $\rho=-0.873179$ with bootstrap 95\% CI [-0.946362, -0.735018]. Both original preregistered co-primary criteria passed.

Subsequent audits tested whether those results uniquely supported the proposed mechanism. A bookkeeping-preserving permutation null tested whether Criterion 2 could arise from recorded-quantity structure, and a downstream-specificity analysis compared selected-action-change rates for near-only and far-only switched contexts.

A prospectively frozen stronger label-preserving control preserved every source row and label, perturbed only \texttt{context\_support\_distance} with zero-mean Gaussian noise, left binary action indicators unchanged, and never perturbed target features. Sixteen noise levels crossed with 16 independent replicates produced 256 candidates. Selection could use only mean exclusion-Jaccard mismatch and total membership-switch mismatch relative to the historical poison condition; target outcomes, regret, selected-action changes, and localization were prohibited from selection. The selected control had mean Jaccard 0.924853 and 304 switches, passing the frozen magnitude gate.

The specificity estimand was $D=$ near membership-switch rate minus far membership-switch rate, with paired contrast $S=D_{poison}-D_{control}$. Exactly 10,000 bootstrap resamples of whole generation seeds were used. Poison enrichment was 0.13623 and matched-control enrichment 0.13438, yielding $S=0.001845$ and 95\% seed-bootstrap CI [0.0000, 0.00554]. The frozen rule required the entire interval to be strictly above zero for poisoning-specific support.

\subsection{Harmful-expansion discrimination and prediction-time audit}
The headline compact model used \texttt{predicted\_loss\_floor}, \texttt{loss\_floor\_error}, and \texttt{expanded\_action\_loss\_error}, where
\begin{align}
\texttt{loss\_floor\_error} &= \texttt{predicted\_loss\_floor}-\texttt{true\_best\_loss},\\
\texttt{expanded\_action\_loss\_error} &= \texttt{predicted\_action\_loss}-\texttt{realized\_expanded\_action\_loss}.
\end{align}
The documented population contained 65 events (15 harmful, 50 beneficial). The compact model reported approximately 0.95 balanced accuracy, 1.00 harmful-event recall, 0.75 precision, and approximately 0.97867 ROC AUC under seed-held-out cross-validation.

A later timing audit established that \texttt{true\_best\_loss} and \texttt{realized\_expanded\_action\_loss} are known only after outcome evaluation. Seed holdout therefore does not make these residuals available before the decision. Models restricted to temporally legitimate loss-surface information showed pooled ROC AUC approximately 0.683--0.711 and balanced accuracy approximately 0.663--0.763; some seed folds contained no harmful events, making fold-level AUC or balanced accuracy undefined.

\subsection{Inferential units and provenance}
Generation seed is the resampling unit when bootstrap uncertainty is reported. Candidate/action rows are nested observations, not independent experimental replications. For harmful expansion, seed-held-out cross-validation controls reuse across generated populations but cannot repair feature-timing violations.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Near-cutoff localization exists in the tested pipeline}
The original poisoning analysis showed strong enrichment of membership switches near the frozen cutoff band. The later non-poison perturbation closely matched exclusion-set overlap and total switching and reproduced nearly the same near-minus-far enrichment. Because the paired confidence interval did not lie strictly above zero, poisoning specificity was not established. This does not prove universal absence of poisoning-specific effects; the present experiment does not distinguish the localization from a sufficiently matched non-poison ranking perturbation.

\subsection{Criterion 2 is not independent mechanistic evidence}
The observed Criterion-2 correlation was reproduced by a bookkeeping-preserving permutation null; the observed $\rho\approx-0.87318$ had one-sided null probability 0.53315. The downstream-specificity analysis also opposed the preferential-near-switch interpretation: selected-action-change rate was 0.63871 for near-only switched contexts and 0.95425 for far-only switched contexts, a difference of -0.31554 with seed-bootstrap 95\% CI [-0.40818, -0.23161].

\subsection{The strongest harmful-expansion model is retrospective}
The compact model's approximately 0.979 ROC AUC shows high outcome-informed retrospective discrimination, not genuine pre-decision prediction, because two residual features require post-outcome information. Temporally legitimate loss-surface-only models showed weaker exploratory discrimination and do not establish prospective or population-generalizable predictive performance.

\section{Negative and Failed Results}
Experiment 165 failed to prospectively replicate an earlier apparent recall $>$ AP $>$ AUC hierarchy. Experiment 158 showed degradation in global prediction metrics but did not satisfy its preregistered prediction-decision divergence criteria. Simple support-distance representations performed poorly as standalone unsafe-behavior detectors, and strong pooled discrimination failed conditioned transfer in at least one action/block setting. Experiment 166's Criterion-2 interpretation did not survive the structural null; preferential downstream near-switch specificity was falsified; poisoning specificity was unresolved under the matched control; and the headline harmful-expansion pre-decision interpretation contains confirmed timing leakage. These findings define the boundary between what the evidence demonstrates and what remains unresolved.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Phenomenon versus poisoning specificity}
Experiment 166 provides evidence for near-cutoff concentration of membership changes in the tested fixed-budget top-N pipeline. The matched non-poison control indicates that the phenomenon can be reproduced without poisoning when ranking perturbation magnitude is closely matched. The strongest defensible interpretation is pipeline-specific cutoff localization, not a poisoning-specific signature. Prior top-K and ranking-stability literature makes boundary sensitivity a relevant competing explanation \cite{Chen2015,Asudeh2018}, but does not establish the Experiment 166 localization as a mathematical necessity or exclude distinct poisoning effects.

\subsection{Association versus mechanism}
The original Criterion-2 correlation cannot be treated as independent mechanistic evidence because a bookkeeping-preserving null reproduces it. The downstream-specificity result also runs opposite the prediction that near-only switches preferentially produce selected-action changes. A causal boundary-composition mechanism remains a possible future hypothesis, not an established result. This distinction is consistent with the broader decision-focused-learning principle that downstream decisions depend on decision-problem structure, not solely generic predictive error \cite{Elmachtoub2022,Wilder2019,Mandi2023}.

\subsection{Retrospective discrimination versus prospective prediction}
High retrospective discrimination can coexist with invalid pre-decision interpretation when predictor construction incorporates quantities realized only after the outcome. Leakage literature provides methodological context \cite{Kapoor2023}; the invalid timing here follows directly from the feature definitions. The temporally legitimate models are therefore the relevant evidence for prospective usefulness and remain exploratory.

\subsection{Scope and generalization}
Conclusions are limited to the studied simulation, ranking rule, intervention-budget structure, models, perturbations, and seeds represented in the committed evidence. The results do not establish deployment validity, cross-domain transfer, aerospace or biomedical effectiveness, or a general law of adaptive systems. Separate adversarial-RL work is not corroborating evidence or mechanistic triangulation for these ADT findings unless a future study explicitly validates such a relationship.

\section{Limitations}
The matched-control result constrains poisoning specificity but does not prove universal absence of poisoning-specific effects. Cutoff localization may be partly or substantially induced by fixed-budget ranking geometry and requires variation of ranking rules or intervention budgets before broader generalization. The harmful-expansion event population is small and imbalanced, and some seed folds lack harmful events. Non-leaking models therefore require stronger prospective evaluation before predictive claims are warranted. The work is simulation-based and does not establish real-world operational validity.

\section{Conclusion}
The evidence supports two narrow conclusions. First, the tested fixed-budget adaptive digital-twin ranking pipeline exhibits robust near-cutoff localization of membership changes, but available structural and matched-control tests do not establish poisoning specificity or an independent causal boundary-composition mechanism. Second, the documented event population shows exploratory discrimination from temporally legitimate loss-surface information, while the strongest compact harmful-expansion result is retrospective because it incorporates post-outcome residuals. Stronger claims of poisoning specificity, causal mechanism, validated prospective prediction, or broad generalization require new discriminating evidence.
